The problem

Why good providers miss out

A council does not visit your service before it scores your bid. It reads what you send. Three things decide most results long before price is even looked at.

1

Location decides

Many contracts only go to providers who can show they already work in that area or can start there safely. Bid outside your patch and you can be ruled out on page one, however strong the rest reads.

2

Scoring decides

Every question carries marks. Buyers score the words in front of them against a marking guide. Care that is real but not written in that shape scores low.

3

Evidence decides

Policies, CQC reports, staffing, training and mobilisation plans all have to line up with each other and with the answers. One mismatch can cost the whole bid.

Learn from other bids

5 mistakes that kill bids

We see the same five errors again and again. Each one is fixable, and most are fixable before you spend a penny on writing.

1

Bidding outside your location

A tender in a borough where you have no staff, no base and no start up plan reads as risk to a buyer. Fix: bid where you can prove cover, travel time and local knowledge, and grow area by area.

2

Treating a framework place as guaranteed work

Getting onto a framework or a DPS gets you the right to be offered work. It does not send work by itself. Fix: plan for call off rounds, mini competitions and direct awards from day one, not after you join.

3

Chasing a contract you cannot win yet

A large single contract that needs turnover, staffing or track record you do not have yet will eat weeks and still fail. Fix: start with small contracts and open DPS routes, build the record, then step up in size.

4

Missing what the buyer needs on CQC

Some routes ask for Good or Outstanding in all domains. Others ask for a clear plan where a report is older or a domain is weaker. Guessing wastes the bid. Fix: read the exact CQC ask in the notice and match your evidence to it before you write a word.

5

Sending documents that do not answer the scoring

Long company histories, copied policies and general claims do not score. Marks go to direct answers with proof. Fix: answer each question in its own shape, with numbers, names, times and evidence the marker can tick off.

A quick test: open your last bid and read only the first line of each answer. If a marker cannot tell what you will do, when, and with what proof from those lines alone, the bid was never going to score well. Our free check reads your position in exactly this way.

Questions

What providers ask us first

We have never bid before. Where do we start?

With the free check and, in most cases, an open DPS route in your own area. It is the lowest risk way to get onto a council list, learn what buyers ask, and build a record you can use in bigger bids later.

Our CQC rating is Requires Improvement. Can we still bid?

For some routes, no, and we will tell you that in the check rather than take a fee. For others, a clear improvement plan and strong evidence in the areas you do well can still be worth a bid. It depends on the exact wording of the notice, which is why the check comes first.

How long does a bid take?

A DPS application with your documents ready can be built in days. A full tender usually needs two to four weeks of work together, and portals often allow four to six weeks in total. Starting at the deadline is the most common avoidable failure, so talk to us as soon as you see a route you like.

Do you only work in the South East?

Our pilot work and deepest live list are in the South East and London, so that is where we can advise best today. If you are elsewhere in the UK, still send the form. If we cannot help well, we will say so and point you to the right official route to watch.
